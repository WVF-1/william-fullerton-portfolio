Fundamental Universal Learning Patterns

A diverse-intelligence-inspired AI training paradigm, grounded in a question: can patterns in how living systems learn help us imagine different ways for artificial systems to develop?

From living learning to artificial systems.

Fundamental Universal Learning Patterns (FULPs) is an independently developed framework that explores learning as a staged developmental process. It asks whether learning patterns observed across living organisms—from simple systems to humans—can inspire alternative approaches to AI training.

The work has moved from a theoretical framework into small proof-of-concept implementations, including a developmental pipeline and experiments with synthetic signals and a water-quality dataset. The early results are preliminary: they suggest a distinct behavior on structural generalization, alongside limitations and cases where the approach performs worse. This is an exploratory research direction, not a claim that the framework is ready to replace existing AI methods.

A recent book brings together the origin story, theory, iterations, and first working version of the framework. It is part research narrative, part technical explanation, and part philosophical exploration.

A measured research story

What the proof of concept explores

The prototype compares staged developmental training with a similarly sized LSTM across controlled test conditions, including a structural generalization task and water-quality data. The paper reports higher mean accuracy on the shifted-pattern task in its experiments, alongside a lower peak in-distribution performance. The result is preliminary and does not establish general superiority.

QuestionCan staged development help a model retain structure when the specific pattern changes?
MethodA proof-of-concept pipeline compared with an LSTM across 30 independent runs.
LimitSmall systems and early experiments; more scaling and validation work is needed.
